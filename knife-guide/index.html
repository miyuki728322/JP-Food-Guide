Introduction · はじめに

A Philosophy of the Edge

刃の哲学

The Japanese kitchen knife is not simply a tool. It is the physical expression of a philosophy: that precision, repetition, and respect for material produce results no shortcut can reach.

Where the Western tradition asks one knife to do everything, the Japanese tradition builds a specific blade for a specific cut — the heavy 出刃 (deba) that breaks down a whole fish, the willow-leaf 柳刃 (yanagiba) that draws a single stroke through a block of tuna, the paper-thin 薄刃 (usuba) that peels a daikon into a continuous translucent sheet.

This guide is written for working chefs. Each chapter stands alone as a reference — jump straight to the steel chart before you buy, or to the sharpening progression when an edge needs repair — but read in order, it is a complete education: the history of the craft, the five essential blades, the steels they are forged from, and the daily disciplines of technique, sharpening, and care that keep a knife alive for decades.

"The knife is the chef's soul. Care for it, and it will speak for you."
切れ味は料理人の魂 — Sakai forging proverb
