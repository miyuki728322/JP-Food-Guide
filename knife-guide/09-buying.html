Budget Reality · 価格帯

What Money Actually Buys

Past ¥15,000, you are no longer buying sharpness — every knife here gets terrifyingly sharp. You are buying edge retention, thinner geometry, and the hands that made it.

〜¥15,000

Entry · 入門

Factory-made モリブデン or entry VG-10 from Seki or Tojiro. Honest geometry, excellent heat treatment, zero romance. This tier outperforms premium Western knives and is the correct place for a first Japanese knife or a whole staff kit.

¥15,000–40,000

Professional · 実用本格

The sweet spot. Hand-finished VG-10, 銀三, and 青紙 from Tosa, Sanjo, and Echizen; real Sakai single bevels begin here. Thinner grinds, better balance, a smith's name on the blade. Most working chefs never need to leave this tier.

¥40,000+

Craft · 名品

Aogami Super and SG2 from named smiths, full Sakai 本霞 single bevels, and — from ¥100,000 up — honyaki. Diminishing returns for the line, real returns for the sashimi station, and heirlooms either way.

Left-handed? · 左利き

Double-bevel knives work as-is. Single-bevel knives (出刃, 柳刃, 薄刃) must be ordered as 左用 (left-handed) — a mirror-image grind, usually made to order at a 30–50% premium. Never buy a right-handed single bevel hoping to adapt; the blade will steer against you on every cut.

At the Counter · 店頭で

Six Checks Before You Pay

A good shop (Sakai's showrooms, Kappabashi in Tokyo, or any serious dealer) will let you handle the knife and will sharpen it before it leaves. Check:

  • Sight down the spine from the handle — the blade must be dead straight, the edge centered (or correctly offset on a single bevel).
  • Check the grind symmetry against the light: the bevel line (刃境) should sweep evenly from heel to tip with no waves.
  • Fit of the ferrule: no gap, no glue smear where 桂 meets blade — that seam is where a wa-handle fails.
  • Balance it in your pinch grip. The point of balance should fall within a finger of where you actually hold it.
  • Ask what steel, exactly. "ステンレス" is not an answer — you want the name: VG-10, 銀三, 青紙二号. If the shop can't say, walk.
  • Ask who sharpens it later. A real dealer offers re-sharpening service (研ぎ直し) — the relationship matters more than the receipt.
