第八章 · Chapter Eight

Regions & Makers

産地と作り手 — Sanchi to Tsukurite

Steel has terroir. Five towns make most of Japan's knives, and each forges to a different philosophy — knowing the town tells you half of what's in the box.

The Forging Towns · 刃物の町

Five Towns, Five Philosophies

堺

Sakai 大阪府 · Osaka

The capital of the professional single bevel. Sakai's six-century-old guild system divides the craft absolutely: a forger (鍛冶屋) shapes and hardens the blade, a separate sharpener (刃付け屋) grinds the geometry, and a third craftsman fits the handle. Each is a master of one thing only — and the result is why roughly nine of ten professional 柳刃 and 薄刃 in Japan carry a Sakai pedigree.

Sakai blades are overwhelmingly carbon steel (白紙, 青紙), traditional, and made to order. If you apprentice at a serious washoku or sushi kitchen, your first real knife will almost certainly come from here.

Signature

Single-bevel mastery · carbon steel · division of labour · made-to-order

堺孝行 Sakai Takayuki 正本 Masamoto 水野鍛錬所 Mizuno Tanrenjo 菊守 Kikumori

関

Seki 岐阜県 · Gifu

Seven centuries ago Seki's water, charcoal, and clay drew the Mino school of swordsmiths; today the same town is Japan's largest blade-manufacturing city — the place where tradition industrialized without losing its head. Seki specializes in precision-made double-bevel stainless: VG-10 gyuto, laser-cut santoku, the knives that introduced the world to Japanese steel.

If Sakai is a tailor's atelier, Seki is a great fashion house: consistent, available, superbly engineered.

Signature

Stainless double bevels · factory precision · global brands · consistency

旬 Shun (貝印 Kai) ミソノ Misono 志津刃物 Shizu Hamono 三星刃物 Mitsuboshi

越前

Echizen 福井県 · Fukui

Seven hundred years of blade-making concentrated today in Takefu Knife Village — a cooperative where independent smiths share a roof, a forge, and a habit of radical experiment. Echizen is also home to Takefu Special Steel, the maker of VG-10 itself, so the town's smiths work the steel they helped invent.

Expect adventurous grinds, Damascus cladding, nashiji finishes, and some of the thinnest "laser" gyuto made anywhere.

Signature

VG-10's birthplace · thin "laser" grinds · smith cooperatives · modern design

高村刃物 Takamura 龍泉刃物 Ryusen 黒崎 Kurosaki 佐治武士 Saji

土佐

Tosa 高知県 · Kōchi

Shikoku's forestry-and-fishing coast bred a blunt, practical forging culture: 自由鍛造 (free forging), hammer-finished blades, and the black 黒打ち (kurouchi) scale finish left deliberately on the blade as honest, rust-shielding workwear. The steel is usually 青紙 — Tosa smiths buy it well and price it kindly.

Tosa knives are the working chef's secret: the best performance-per-yen in carbon steel, with looks that improve as they wear.

Signature

Kurouchi rustic finish · 青紙 carbon · free forging · honest prices

土佐打刃物 Tosa Uchihamono 田中打刃物 Tanaka 山下刃物 Yamashita

三条

Sanjō 新潟県 · Niigata

In the Tsubame-Sanjo metalworking belt, knife makers do something unusual in Japan: everything under one roof. Companies like Tadafusa and Yoshikane forge, grind, sharpen, and handle in-house, which breeds coherent, ground-up design — distinctive profiles, supremely even thin grinds, and a willingness to pair traditional kurouchi looks with modern stainless-clad construction.

Sanjo gyuto and santoku are favorites of chefs who want one maker's intent in the whole knife.

Signature

Integrated makers · stainless-clad carbon · refined thin grinds · design coherence

Cheat Sheet · 早見表

Which Town for Which Knife

Single-bevel for the sushi station → 堺 Sakai. Dependable stainless gyuto for the line → 関 Seki. The thinnest modern grinds → 越前 Echizen. Carbon steel on a budget → 土佐 Tosa. A maker's complete vision → 三条 Sanjō.
