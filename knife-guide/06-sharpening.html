Understand the lift · 背の高さ

Spine height = blade width × sin(angle). A 45 mm blade at 15° needs approximately 11.6 mm of lift. Two 1.5 mm coins provide only 3 mm of lift — about 3.8°, not 15°. This simplified model measures from the cutting edge to the spine and excludes blade thickness.

The angle is shown for one side. Follow your knife maker’s guidance; single-bevel knives should follow their existing bevel rather than a generic preset.

The Whetstone Progression · 砥石の番手

From Repair to Mirror

Grit numbers run from violent to gentle. You do not need all seven stages — a working chef lives at #1000 and visits #3000 weekly. The red stages are the essential pair.

Stage 1 #120–220荒砥 — arato Repair territory. Chips, broken tips, and profile changes. Removes steel fast enough to ruin a blade in careless hands — count your strokes and check constantly.
Stage 2 #400–600荒砥 — coarse Heavy reshaping after repair, or rescuing a truly neglected edge. Leaves deep scratches the next stones must erase.
Stage 3 ● #800–1200中砥 — nakato The home base. The #1000 stone is where 90% of all sharpening happens — it raises a complete, keen, working edge on its own. If you own one stone, it is this one.
Stage 4 #2000中砥 — fine middle A refining bridge. Erases #1000 scratches and tightens the edge before polish. Optional but pleasant.
Stage 5 ● #3000–4000仕上砥 — shiageto The professional finish. The edge stops feeling toothy and starts feeling silent. For most line work, stopping here gives the best balance of bite and polish.
Stage 6 #6000–8000仕上砥 — fine finish For single-bevel work: sashimi and katsuramuki edges that must leave a gleaming cut surface. A 柳刃 lives here.
Stage 7 #10000+鏡面 — mirror Ceremony and showmanship — plus genuinely less cell damage on raw fish. Beyond #8000, gains are about pride as much as performance. 天然砥石 (natural stones) play here.

Know Your Stone · 砥石の種類

Three Kinds of Stone

吸水砥石

Soaking water stones

The classic. Soak 5–10 minutes until the bubbles stop, then sharpen on the slurry that forms. Soft, fast-cutting, wears hollow — flatten regularly with a lapping plate. King and Suehiro are the canonical brands.

不吸水砥石

Splash-and-go

Hard ceramic bonds that need only a wet surface — no soak, ready in seconds mid-service. Cut slower but stay flat far longer. Shapton Glass is the professional default. Best for hard powder steels like SG2.

天然砥石

Natural stones

Quarried, mostly around Kyoto; no two identical. Their irregular particles leave a hazy kasumi finish and a uniquely toothy-yet-fine edge prized for single-bevel knives. Expensive, subtle, and a lifetime rabbit hole.

Discipline · 研ぎの七則

The Seven Rules

  1. Soak soaking stones 5–10 minutes; splash-and-go stones need only a wet surface. A dry stone glazes and burns the edge.
  2. Keep the angle; vary nothing. A wobbling wrist rounds the edge. Lock your elbow to your hip and move from the shoulders.
  3. Pressure on the push, feather on the return. Two fingers of the free hand ride the blade above the section being ground.
  4. Work in zones. Heel, belly, tip — ten strokes each, then check. The burr (かえり, kaeri) forming on the far side tells you the zone is done.
  5. Chase the burr, then remove it. Sharpen until a burr runs the full edge, flip and repeat, then strip it with light alternating strokes or on newspaper.
  6. Flatten your stones. A dished stone makes a convex lie out of every angle you hold. Lap with a diamond plate before every session.
  7. Finish single-bevels on the 裏 (back) with the blade dead flat — two or three strokes only. The urasuki is sacred; grinding it ruins the knife.
